Before you begin

How long is 4.2 billion years?

This page draws all of that time as one unbroken line. Every pixel is a thousand years, so the line is 4.2 million pixels long. That is far wider than any screen, so it is wound into a spiral. It starts at the centre in the deep past and ends at the top at the year 0 AD. From there a short straight strip runs right to today, at one pixel per ten years, which is 100 times more magnified than the spiral.

The aim is to show the span of time that we can trace, and to let you see how small a place our own existence takes up in it.

Squeeze it into one day

If 4.2 billion years were a single day ending at midnight today, one second would stand for about 48,600 years.

The line starts 4.2 billion years ago, a round figure inside Earth's age of about 4.54 billion years. Dates for older periods are approximate.

Help

How to use the map

Reading the spiral

Start
The line begins at the centre, 4.2 billion years ago, and winds outward. Later times sit further from the centre.
End
The spiral ends at the top, at the year 0 AD. A straight strip then runs to the right, to today, with one pixel for every ten years. That is 100 times more magnified than the spiral. Hover the magnifier at its right end for a reminder.
Colours
White is the timeline. Gold is the period you picked.
Scale
The bar at the top left shows how many years a stretch of screen covers at the current zoom. Grey labels along the line mark years ago.

Highlighting a period

Dropdown
Choose a period under "Highlight a period". Its stretch of the line turns gold, and the card at the bottom gives its dates, duration and share of the whole timeline.
Short periods
Some are only a few pixels long at full view. The map moves in to show them and rings the spot in gold.
Zoom to period
Fits the highlighted period on screen.
Whole spiral
Returns to the full view.
Modern years
Jumps to the straight strip from 0 AD to today.

Moving around

Pan
Drag the map.
Zoom
Scroll the wheel, pinch on a touch screen, or use the + and − buttons.
Read a date
Move over the line, or tap it, to see the approximate date at that point. It appears at the top left.
Keyboard
Click the map, then use ←→↑↓ to pan, + and − to zoom, and 0 for the whole spiral.
